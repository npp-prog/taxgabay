Estate tax is 6% of the net estate of a person who died on or after 1 January 2018. The net estate is the gross estate less ordinary deductions, the ₱5,000,000 standard deduction, the family home (up to ₱10,000,000) and the surviving spouse's share.

## Who must file

- The executor or administrator of the estate, or any of the legal heirs

## Deadline

- Within **one year from the date of death**. Extensions of filing and payment may be granted in meritorious cases (to confirm).

## Where to file and pay

- **File:** eBIRForms where available, or with the RDO that has jurisdiction (for one-time transactions, usually where the property is located or where the decedent/donor resided — to confirm).
- **Pay:** authorized agent banks, BIR-accredited e-payment channels, or the Revenue Collection Officer of an RDO (under the Ease of Paying Taxes Act you may file and pay anywhere — to confirm for one-time transactions, which may still be processed by the RDO where the property is located).

## Attachments

- Death certificate
- Titles, tax declarations and proof of fair market value of each property at the time of death
- Proof of claims, unpaid mortgages and other deductions
- Proof of the family home and of the relationship of the heirs
- Other documents required for the eCAR

## Common mistakes

- Valuing property at purchase price instead of fair market value at death.
- Deducting the whole conjugal family home instead of the decedent's share (to confirm).
- Missing the one-year deadline, which adds surcharge and interest.
