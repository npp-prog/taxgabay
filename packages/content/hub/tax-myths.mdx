## Myth: "If I earn less than ₱250,000, I don't need to register or file."

**Fact:** The ₱250,000 is a tax exemption amount, not an exemption from registering or filing. If you have a business or profession, you still register and file your returns — you may simply owe no income tax.

## Myth: "No sales this quarter means no return."

**Fact:** You still file. Returns with no tax due are still required, and filing late carries penalties.

## Myth: "Freelancers paid online don't need to register."

**Fact:** Self-employed individuals and professionals register with the BIR like any other business, wherever their clients are.

## Myth: "The 8% option is always cheaper."

**Fact:** It often is when expenses are low, but graduated rates with itemized deductions can be cheaper when expenses are high. Compare first with the income tax calculator — the election is irrevocable for the year.

## Myth: "Employees never file anything."

**Fact:** Employees with one employer and correctly withheld tax usually qualify for substituted filing, where Form 2316 serves as the return. But employees with a side business or a second employer during the year may need to file their own annual return.

## Myth: "I still have to pay the ₱500 annual registration fee."

**Fact:** The annual registration fee was abolished by the Ease of Paying Taxes Act.

## Myth: "Official receipts are what I need to issue for services."

**Fact:** Under the Ease of Paying Taxes Act, the sales invoice is the main document for both goods and services; official receipts are now supplementary.
