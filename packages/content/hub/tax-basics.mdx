Starting a business means you now have obligations with the BIR (Bureau of Internal Revenue), the government agency that collects national taxes. The good news: most of them repeat on a fixed schedule, so once you set up, it becomes routine.

## 1. Register before you start operating

- Register your business name or entity first: DTI for a sole proprietorship, SEC for a partnership, corporation or OPC (One Person Corporation), or CDA for a cooperative.
- Get a barangay clearance and a mayor's (business) permit from your city or municipality.
- Register with the BIR: Form 1901 for individuals, Form 1903 for corporations and partnerships. You can use ORUS (the BIR's Online Registration and Update System) or go to your RDO (Revenue District Office), the BIR office for your area.
- You will receive a Certificate of Registration (COR, Form 2303). It lists your "tax types" — the taxes and returns you are responsible for.
- The ₱500 annual registration fee was abolished by the Ease of Paying Taxes Act (EOPT).

## 2. Keep books of accounts

- Register your books (manual, loose-leaf or computerized) and record every sale and expense.
- Update them as you go — not just before filing.

## 3. Issue invoices for every sale

- Under the EOPT, the sales invoice is the main document for both goods and services. Official receipts are now supplementary.
- Get an Authority to Print from an accredited printer, or register your invoicing system.
- Display your COR and the "Ask for an invoice" notice where customers can see them.

## 4. File returns on time, even with zero sales

- Most small businesses file a quarterly income tax return and either a quarterly percentage tax return (non-VAT) or a quarterly VAT return (VAT-registered), plus an annual income tax return.
- If you have employees, or pay rent, professional fees or contractors, you also have withholding tax returns.
- File even when there was no income for the period — late filing carries penalties even with no tax due.
- Your TaxGabay calendar lists your deadlines and moves them to the next working day when they fall on a weekend or holiday.

## 5. Keep your records

- Keep your books, invoices, returns, proof of payment and certificates such as BIR Form 2307 for the period required by law.

## Legal basis

- NIRC Sec. 236, as amended by RA 11976 (EOPT) — registration
- NIRC Sec. 232 — books of accounts
- NIRC Secs. 113 and 237, as amended by RA 11976 — invoicing
