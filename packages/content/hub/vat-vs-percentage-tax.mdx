Besides income tax, most businesses pay a "business tax" on their sales: either VAT (value-added tax) or percentage tax. Your Certificate of Registration (COR, Form 2303) shows which one is yours.

## VAT (value-added tax)

- 12% added to the price of your vatable sales. This is your output VAT.
- You subtract the VAT you paid on your business purchases (input VAT), supported by valid VAT invoices.
- You pay the difference. If input VAT is higher, the excess is carried over to the next quarter.
- File Form 2550Q within 25 days after the end of each quarter.

## Percentage tax

- 3% of your gross sales or receipts for the quarter, with no deduction for purchases.
- It was temporarily 1% from 1 July 2020 to 30 June 2023 under the CREATE law.
- File Form 2551Q within 25 days after the end of each quarter.
- If you chose the 8% income tax option, you do not pay percentage tax — the 8% replaces it.

## The ₱3 million threshold

- You must register for VAT once your gross sales or receipts for the year exceed ₱3,000,000.
- Below that, you are non-VAT and pay percentage tax, unless you register for VAT voluntarily.

## Which is better?

- VAT suits businesses that sell mostly to other VAT-registered businesses (they can claim your VAT as input tax) or that have large VAT-able purchases.
- Percentage tax is simpler and suits small businesses selling to consumers.
- Being VAT-registered also means you cannot use the 8% income tax option.

## Legal basis

- NIRC Secs. 106 and 108, as amended by RA 9337 — 12% VAT
- NIRC Sec. 109, as amended by RA 10963 (TRAIN) — ₱3M threshold
- NIRC Sec. 116, as amended by RA 11534 (CREATE) — percentage tax
