If you are self-employed, a professional, a sole proprietor or a mixed-income earner (an employee who also has business or professional income), you choose how your business income is taxed.

## Option 1: Graduated rates

- Your net taxable income is taxed at rates from 0% to 35%; the first ₱250,000 is not taxed.
- To get net income, you deduct either:
  - the Optional Standard Deduction (OSD): 40% of your gross sales or receipts, with no receipts needed for expenses; or
  - itemized deductions: your actual business expenses, supported by receipts and books.
- If you are not VAT-registered, you also pay percentage tax (3%) on your gross sales every quarter.

## Option 2: The 8% option

- 8% of your gross sales or receipts and other non-operating income.
- It replaces both the graduated income tax on that income and the percentage tax.
- If you are purely self-employed, you first deduct ₱250,000. Mixed-income earners do not get this reduction, because their compensation is taxed separately at graduated rates.

## Who can choose 8%

- You must not be VAT-registered.
- Your gross sales or receipts and other non-operating income must not exceed ₱3,000,000 for the year.
- Corporations and partnerships cannot use it.

## When you must decide

- Elect the 8% option in your first quarterly income tax return of the year (Form 1701Q, due 15 May) or when you register.
- The choice is irrevocable for that year.

## How to compare

- The 8% option usually wins when your expenses are low compared to your sales.
- Graduated rates with itemized deductions can win when your expenses are high and well documented.
- Use the income tax calculator: it compares all options side by side, including percentage tax, and shows the legal basis for each line.
