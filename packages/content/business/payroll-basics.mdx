## The steps each payday

1. **Gross pay**: basic salary plus taxable allowances for the period.
2. **Less employee contributions**: SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG employee shares.
3. **Less non-taxable pay**: de minimis benefits within their ceilings.
4. **Taxable compensation** = gross pay − contributions − non-taxable pay.
5. **Withholding tax**: look up the taxable compensation in the revised withholding tax table for the pay period (monthly, semi-monthly, weekly or daily).
6. **Net pay** = gross pay − employee contributions − withholding tax − other deductions (loans, etc.).

The employer also pays its own share of SSS, PhilHealth and Pag-IBIG on top of the salary.

## Contribution rates in TaxGabay (to confirm)

- **SSS**: 15% of the monthly salary credit from 2025 — 10% employer, 5% employee — with the salary credit between ₱5,000 and ₱35,000.
- **PhilHealth**: 5% of monthly basic salary, shared equally, on salary between ₱10,000 and ₱100,000.
- **Pag-IBIG**: 2% employee (1% if earning ₱1,500 or less) and 2% employer, on salary up to ₱10,000.

Example for a ₱20,000 monthly salary in 2026: employee pays ₱1,000 SSS + ₱500 PhilHealth + ₱200 Pag-IBIG = ₱1,700; the employer adds ₱2,000 + ₱500 + ₱200 = ₱2,700.

## Withholding tax example

Monthly taxable compensation of ₱25,000 (2023 table onwards): 15% of the excess over ₱20,833 = ₱625.05. Use the calculator for other amounts.

## 13th month pay

- Equal to 1/12 of the total basic salary earned in the calendar year, paid on or before 24 December (to confirm coverage).
- 13th month pay and other benefits are tax-free up to ₱90,000 a year; the excess is taxable.

## De minimis benefits

Small benefits within ceilings are not taxable, for example a rice subsidy of up to ₱2,000 a month or a uniform allowance of up to ₱6,000 a year (ceilings to confirm).

## Payslips

Give each employee a payslip showing gross pay, each deduction and net pay.
