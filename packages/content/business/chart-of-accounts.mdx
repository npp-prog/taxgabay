A chart of accounts is the list of accounts you use in your books, each with a number. It decides how your financial statements are grouped, so keep it short and consistent.

## How it is organized

Accounts are grouped in the order they appear in the financial statements:

- **1xx Assets**: what the business owns (cash, receivables, equipment).
- **2xx Liabilities**: what it owes (suppliers, taxes payable).
- **3xx Equity**: the owner's investment and withdrawals, or a corporation's capital and retained earnings.
- **4xx Revenue**: income from sales or services.
- **5xx Expenses**: costs of running the business.

Some accounts reduce another account, such as Accumulated Depreciation (reduces Equipment) or the owner's Drawing (reduces Capital). These are called contra accounts.

## Starter chart: small service business

- 101 Cash on Hand
- 102 Cash in Bank
- 110 Accounts Receivable
- 115 Creditable Withholding Tax (from Forms 2307)
- 150 Equipment
- 151 Accumulated Depreciation – Equipment
- 201 Accounts Payable
- 301 Owner, Capital
- 302 Owner, Drawing
- 401 Service Income (or Professional Fees)
- 501 Rent Expense
- 502 Utilities Expense
- 503 Supplies Expense
- 504 Taxes and Licenses
- 505 Depreciation Expense

## Starter chart: small store

Add these to the service chart:

- 120 Merchandise Inventory
- 401 Sales
- 402 Sales Returns and Allowances
- 410 Cost of Sales (or Purchases, if you count inventory only at year end)

Stores that sell goods also submit a year-end inventory list (see your annual checklist).

## Tips

- Use the same account names every month.
- Do not mix personal spending into business accounts; record it as Drawing.
