A bank reconciliation checks that your **Cash in Bank** account and the **bank statement** agree. Differences usually come from timing or from items only the bank knows about.

## The five steps

1. Get the bank statement and your Cash in Bank ledger for the same month.
2. Tick each deposit and payment that appears in both.
3. List items in your books but not yet in the bank: **deposits in transit** and **outstanding checks**.
4. List items in the bank but not yet in your books: **service charges**, **interest earned**, and any errors.
5. Adjust both sides and check that they agree. Record entries in your books for the bank-only items.

## Worked example

End of month:

- Your Cash in Bank account shows ₱50,000.
- The bank statement shows ₱47,850.
- A ₱7,000 deposit made on the last day is not yet on the statement (deposit in transit).
- Check No. 102 for ₱5,000 to a supplier has not yet been cashed (outstanding check).
- The bank deducted ₱150 in service charges that you have not recorded.

Bank side: ₱47,850 + ₱7,000 − ₱5,000 = **₱49,850**.

Book side: ₱50,000 − ₱150 = **₱49,850**.

Both agree. Record the ₱150 in your books: debit Bank Charges, credit Cash in Bank.

## Tips

- Reconcile every month, before you prepare your returns.
- Investigate any difference you cannot explain; do not force the numbers.
