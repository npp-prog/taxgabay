Every registered business keeps books of accounts: the records of each sale, purchase, payment and receipt that support your tax returns. The BIR registers your books before you use them.

## Which books

Most small businesses keep these:

- **General journal**: entries that do not fit the other books, such as opening balances, adjustments and depreciation.
- **General ledger**: one page (account) for each item in your chart of accounts, such as Cash, Sales or Rent Expense. Entries from the journals are *posted* here.
- **Cash receipts book**: every amount of cash or bank money you receive.
- **Cash disbursements book**: every payment you make.
- **Sales journal**: sales made on credit (billed now, paid later).
- **Purchase journal**: purchases made on credit.

Small taxpayers may keep simplified books, as allowed for their size (to confirm with your RDO).

## Manual, loose-leaf or computerized

- **Manual (bound) books**: columnar notebooks you buy, register, and write in by hand.
- **Loose-leaf books**: printed pages from a spreadsheet or program, bound at the end of the year.
- **Computerized books**: an accounting system registered with the BIR.

Whichever you use, register it through ORUS or your RDO before the first entry (see the registration roadmap).

## How often to update

- Record transactions as they happen, or at least weekly. Your monthly checklist reminds you to update your books every month.
- At the end of each year, close the books and bind loose-leaf pages early in the following year (to confirm the deadline).

## Keeping records

- Keep your books, invoices, receipts, Forms 2307 and bank statements together for each period.
- The law requires books and records to be kept for a number of years — modeled here as 5 years (LOW CONFIDENCE, to confirm).
- Books must be available if the BIR examines your returns.
