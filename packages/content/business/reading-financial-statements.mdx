Financial statements summarize your books. They come straight from your ledger accounts.

## Income statement

Shows how much you earned and spent over a period.

- Revenue − cost of sales = gross profit.
- Gross profit − operating expenses = net income (or loss).

## Statement of changes in equity

Shows how the owner's stake changed:

- Opening capital + net income − withdrawals (or dividends) = ending capital.

## Balance sheet

Shows what the business owns and owes on one date:

- Assets = liabilities + equity. If they don't agree, something is wrong in the books.

## Small example

From the freelance designer practice set (first quarter):

- Revenue (professional fees): ₱136,000
- Expenses: ₱48,900
- **Net income: ₱87,100**
- Capital at the start: ₱155,000; withdrawals: ₱15,000
- **Ending capital: ₱155,000 + ₱87,100 − ₱15,000 = ₱227,100**
- Total assets at the end: ₱227,100 — equal to liabilities (none) plus equity.

## What to look at

- Is net income positive, and is it growing?
- Do you have enough cash for upcoming taxes and bills?
- Are receivables (unpaid invoices) growing faster than sales?

## Legal basis

- NIRC Sec. 232, as amended (books of accounts)
